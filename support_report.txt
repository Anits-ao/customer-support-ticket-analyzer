# CUSTOMER SUPPORT TICKET ANALYSIS REPORT

## Project Overview

This project analyzes 4,000 customer support tickets to examine support team performance, response times, ticket resolution records, ticket reopening patterns, and customer satisfaction.

## Dataset Summary

- Total tickets analyzed: 4,000
- Support queues: 5
- Priority levels: 4
- Tickets with recorded first-response times: 3,851
- Tickets with recorded resolution timestamps: 3,775
- Tickets with customer satisfaction ratings: 1,226

## Key Performance Findings

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Average customer satisfaction | 3.99/5 |
| Tickets with recorded resolution timestamps | 94.38% |
| Ticket reopening rate | 7.12% |
| Missing first-response times | 149 |
| Missing CSAT ratings | 2,774 |
| Valid CSAT ratings | 1,226 |
| Invalid CSAT ratings | 0 |

## Average First Response Time by Priority

| Priority | Average response time |
|---|---:|
| High | 68.46 minutes |
| Normal | 149.50 minutes |
| Urgent | 25.11 minutes |
| Low | 302.62 minutes |

## Customer Satisfaction by Support Queue

| Support queue | Average CSAT |
|---|---:|
| Integrations | 4.24/5 |
| Account | 4.08/5 |
| Onboarding | 4.01/5 |
| Billing | 3.95/5 |
| Technical | 3.85/5 |

## Key Observations

1. The integrations queue recorded the highest average customer satisfaction score at 4.24/5.
2. The technical queue recorded the lowest average satisfaction score at 3.85/5.
3. Urgent tickets had the shortest average first-response time, while low-priority tickets had the longest.
4. Approximately 7.12% of tickets with recognized reopening statuses were reopened.
5. Customer satisfaction data was missing for 69.35% of all tickets, limiting the coverage of satisfaction-based findings.

## Data Quality and Limitations

- 149 tickets had missing first-response times.
- 225 tickets had no recorded resolution timestamp.
- 2,774 tickets had no customer satisfaction rating.
- All 1,226 recorded CSAT ratings passed the numerical validation check of 1–5.
- The dataset does not establish why response times, resolution timestamps, or satisfaction ratings are missing.
- These findings describe patterns in the supplied dataset and should not be interpreted as proof of causal relationships.

## Conclusion

The analysis highlights differences in response times and customer satisfaction across support priorities and queues. It also identifies data completeness as an important consideration when evaluating customer support performance.

The findings can help identify areas for further investigation, particularly the technical support queue and the high volume of missing customer satisfaction ratings.
